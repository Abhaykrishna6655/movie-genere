ACADEMIC PROJECT REPORT: MOVIE GENRE CLASSIFICATION
Predicting Movie Genres from Plot Summaries using TF-IDF & Multinomial Naive Bayes
Course: Machine Learning & Natural Language Processing
Date: Academic Term 2026
================================================================================

1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
With hundreds of thousands of motion pictures released globally, streaming platforms 
and digital archives require automated categorization mechanisms to organize large 
media catalogs, enhance search indexing, and power personalized recommendation systems. 
Manual genre tagging is labor-intensive, subjective, and prone to inconsistency. 
This project addresses the challenge of automated text classification: given an 
unstructured textual plot summary of a movie, build and evaluate a machine learning 
pipeline to accurately predict its primary cinematic genre (e.g., Action, Comedy, 
Drama, Horror, Romance, Sci-Fi, Thriller).

2. TEXT DATA PREPROCESSING & FEATURE EXTRACTION (TF-IDF)
Raw movie plot descriptions consist of unstructured natural language containing noise, 
syntactic variations, and non-informative tokens. The preprocessing pipeline applies:
  a. Text Normalization: Conversion of all characters to lowercase to eliminate case 
     sensitivity (e.g., "Alien" vs. "alien").
  b. Noise Reduction: Regular-expression removal of HTML markup, URLs, numerical 
     digits, and punctuation characters.
  c. Stop Word Removal: Elimination of high-frequency English functional words 
     (such as "the", "and", "is", "with") that carry minimal genre-specific discriminative 
     information.
  d. Tokenization & N-gram Extraction: Splitting text into individual tokens as well 
     as bi-grams (1, 2) to preserve meaningful domain phrases (e.g., "serial killer", 
     "deep space", "romantic dinner").
  e. TF-IDF Vectorization: Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) transforms 
     the variable-length text into high-dimensional numerical feature vectors:
         TF-IDF(t, d, D) = TF(t, d) * log((1 + |D|) / (1 + DF(t))) + 1
     This penalizes universally ubiquitous terms while rewarding words that are 
     distinctive to specific genres. A vocabulary threshold of the top 5,000 features 
     with sublinear TF scaling is implemented to control sparsity and prevent term saturation.

3. ALGORITHM SELECTION: WHY MULTINOMIAL NAIVE BAYES FOR TEXT?
The Multinomial Naive Bayes (MNB) classifier was selected as the optimal algorithm for 
this task due to several theoretical and empirical strengths in Natural Language Processing:
  a. High-Dimensional Text Suitability: Text classification involves thousands of feature 
     dimensions. Naive Bayes performs exceptionally well in high-dimensional, sparse 
     vector spaces where complex discriminative models often overfit.
  b. Multinomial Distribution Match: MNB is mathematically formulated to model discrete 
     event counts and word frequency distributions (and by extension TF-IDF weights) 
     occurring in text documents.
  c. Computational Efficiency: Training and inference exhibit linear time complexity 
     O(N * |V|), making it orders of magnitude faster to train and deploy than support 
     vector machines or recurrent neural networks.
  d. Laplace Smoothing (alpha = 0.5): Prevents zero-probability penalties for unseen 
     vocabulary terms during testing, maintaining robust probabilistic calibration.

4. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP & EVALUATION METRICS
The dataset was partitioned into an 80% training set and a 20% test set using 
stratified sampling to preserve class distribution across genres. Model performance 
was evaluated using the following standard statistical metrics:
  - Accuracy: Overall proportion of correct predictions across all test instances.
  - Precision: Proportion of predicted instances for a genre that were truly correct, 
    minimizing false positives.
  - Recall: Sensitivity metric measuring the proportion of actual genre instances correctly 
    identified, minimizing false negatives.
  - F1-Score (Macro & Weighted): Harmonic mean of precision and recall, balancing 
    performance across both majority and minority genre classes.
  - Confusion Matrix: Inter-class confusion tracking to observe genre misclassifications.

5. ANALYSIS OF RESULTS & OBSERVATIONS
Experimental evaluation revealed the following key insights:
  a. Lexical Distinctiveness: Genres with unique, highly specialized vocabularies—such 
     as Horror ("ghost", "curse", "haunted", "demonic"), Sci-Fi ("spaceship", "alien", 
     "planet", "future"), and Romance ("love", "wedding", "passion")—achieved the highest 
     individual precision and recall scores.
  b. Multi-Genre Overlap: Genres with broader narrative thematic scope, particularly 
     Drama and Thriller, exhibited higher misclassification rates. Because a dramatic plot 
     can easily overlap with crime, romance, or action themes, single-label classification 
     faces inherent linguistic ambiguity.
  c. Feature Sparsity Management: Incorporating sublinear term frequency scaling and 
     bi-grams significantly improved discrimination between nuanced sub-genres compared 
     to basic bag-of-words count vectorization.

6. LIMITATIONS & FUTURE SCOPE
While Multinomial Naive Bayes with TF-IDF provides a lightweight, highly interpretable, 
and low-latency baseline, real-world movie summaries frequently belong to multiple genres 
simultaneously (e.g., Sci-Fi Comedy). Future enhancements include:
  1. Multi-Label Classification: Transitioning to Binary Relevance or Classifier Chains 
     to predict multi-genre tags per movie.
  2. Contextual Transformer Embeddings: Implementing contextual language models (such as 
     BERT or RoBERTa) to capture semantic dependencies, polysemy, and narrative structure 
     beyond keyword occurrence.

7. CONCLUSION
The end-to-end pipeline demonstrates that TF-IDF feature extraction combined with a 
Multinomial Naive Bayes classifier delivers an effective, robust, and computationally 
efficient solution for movie genre prediction from synopsis text, fulfilling all academic 
and production deployment objectives.
